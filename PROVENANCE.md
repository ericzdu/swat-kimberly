# Where every part of this model came from

Every value in `model/TxtInOut` traced to the **specific source file** it was ported from, and
the script that did it. This exists because the calibration argument in `PAPER.md` depends on
it: a model whose inputs cannot be traced cannot support a claim about what its residual error
means.

Audit generated by diffing against a clean `a10_single_hru` template. Of the 65 files SWAT+
actually reads, **25 are modified, 2 are new, and 38 are untouched template defaults.**

---

## The four source locations

| tag | location on disk | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **REF** | `~/Downloads/TxtInOut-2/` | calibrated **ArcSWAT / SWAT2012** model of this field. 2,461 files, 330 HRUs, 16 subbasins. Our field is **HRU 119 / subbasin 14** → files `0001400xx.*`. Reproduces GRACEnet yields to +5.1 % PBIAS |
| **RUFAS** | `~/Desktop/projects/RuFaS/input/data/` | the RuFaS model of the same field, scenario `kimberly_rotation` |
| **A10** | `~/Downloads/a10_single_hru/Scenarios/Default/TxtInOut/` | SWAT+ demo template — the starting point |
| **MEAS** | `~/Documents/Kimberly, Idaho/` | **the primary data for this site** — the canonical folder. REF and RUFAS are both downstream of it. As of 2026-07-28 the ports read this directly wherever possible |

A note on A10: it is not a foreign watershed. Its weather-generator database is a 3×3 grid of
stations spanning 42.31–42.93 °N, 114.06–114.69 °W — the Twin Falls/Kimberly region itself.
That is why it was chosen. It is still a demo dataset, and everything marked A10 below is an
unexamined default.

A note on REF's file numbering: SWAT2012 names per-HRU files `SSSSSHHHH.ext` where `SSSSS` is
the 5-digit subbasin and `HHHH` the 4-digit HRU-within-subbasin. Subbasin-level files use HRU
`0000`. So subbasin 14's files are `000140000.*` and its first HRU is `000140001.*`.

**The model's irrigation is the GRACEnet measurement, to 0.1 mm in every year, and the event
counts sum to exactly the 158 in `management.sch`.** RuFaS was a faithful conduit, not a
distortion — the value is measured, and the path it travelled does not change that. Where the
two disagree slightly it is the *reference* that departs from the measurement, not us: 2014
(418.3 vs 400.8) and 2019 (502.4 vs 520.8).

The 2019 row read **520.7 mm** here until 2026-07-31, which was wrong — that is the
*reference's* figure, not the extraction's. `data/irrigation_gracenet.csv` has 20 events at
24.384 mm and 31.75 mm, summing to 502.412 mm, and that is what `management.sch` applies.
Worth noting *why* they differ: 24.384 mm is exactly 0.96 in and 31.75 mm exactly 1.25 in,
where the reference used round 1.00 in and 1.25 in. So the reference rounded the 2019 set
depth up to the nearest inch and the extraction did not. The extraction is the measurement.

`port_management.py` could be repointed at the spreadsheets to remove the RuFaS dependency
entirely. Worth doing for reproducibility; it would not change a number.

### 1b. 2020 irrigation exists, and neither model uses it

`Irrigation 2020-2022.xlsx`, sheet `2020`, records **26 events totalling 757.7 mm**, running
22 April to 11 October. The model applies 0 mm; the reference auto-irrigates 304.8 mm.

This does **not** simply resolve the 2020 gap, because the model's 2020 is not the field's 2020.
Both this model and the reference treat 2020 as a repeat of the 2012 barley spin-up year — a
modelling convenience for closing the 8-year rotation over a 9-year window. The measured
season runs into October with heavy August applications, which is not a spring-barley pattern
(that crop is harvested in August, and the site's own documented barley irrigation window ends
7/25). So the measured 757.7 mm belongs to a different crop than the one the model simulates
in 2020.

**Resolved 2026-07-28: option 1. `time.sim` now ends at 2019** (`port_management.SIM_END_YEAR`).

Three options were open:
1. **Drop 2020 from the simulation.** Neither model represents the real 2020, so scoring
   against it measures nothing. Cheapest and most defensible. ← chosen
2. **Simulate the real 2020** — identify the actual crop from the GRACEnet records and schedule
   it, using the measured 757.7 mm. Adds a genuine year of evidence.
3. **Keep the synthetic barley year** and adopt the reference's auto-irrigation for comparability.

What forced the decision was that 2020 was not merely unused, it was actively being *scored*.
`print.prt` skips only the 2012 spin-up, so a fabricated barley year receiving **zero
irrigation** in a year when 757.7 mm was applied entered every tier-2 statistic. Its simulated
ET came out at 250 mm against 421–1013 mm in every other year. Option 2 remains the better
science and stays available — the 26 events are still extracted to
`data/irrigation_gracenet.csv` — but it needs the crop identified first, and option 3 would
have kept a fabricated crop *and* fabricated water. What remains is a 2012 spin-up plus
2013–2019, every one of which has a measured yield.

Note this overturns a claim in `EXPERIMENTS.md`: wiring auto-irrigation was described there as
"required for correctness" because 2020 got 0 mm. Measured 2020 irrigation exists, so
auto-irrigation is a *gym design* requirement, not a calibration fix.

## 2. Crop parameters: the site's own SWAT table, not RuFaS's

`port_crops.py` reads RuFaS's `kimberly_rotation_crop_configs.json` and overwrites 20 columns
of `plants.plt` per crop. But those values did not originate with RuFaS. They are the
**site's calibrated SWAT crop parameter table**, held at
`~/Documents/Kimberly, Idaho/Crop Parameters and Plant Harvest dates.xlsx`, sheet
`SWAT Crop Parameters`. RuFaS is a conduit; the numbers are the same.

That sheet holds two blocks. The first is the Kimberly-calibrated set, the second the stock
SWAT database defaults — which is what makes it useful as a plausibility anchor:

| crop | | `BIO_E` calibrated | `BIO_E` SWAT default | `HVSTI` | `BLAI` | `T_OPT` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ALFA` | Alfalfa | 17.0 | 20.0 | 1.00 | 5.0 | 20.0 |
| `CSIL` | Corn Silage | 50.0 | 39.0 | 1.01 | 4.0 | 25.0 |
| `BARL` | **Spring Barley** | 28.0 | 35.0 | 0.52 | 4.0 | 25.0 |

Every one of these matches what is in `plants.plt` today.

**Correction to an earlier version of this document.** It stated that the model's barley was
parameterized from RuFaS's *triticale silage* configuration, and flagged that as a candidate
explanation for the barley gap. That is wrong: RuFaS merely files the Kimberly **Spring Barley
(`BARL`)** parameters under a config named `triticale_silage`. The values are barley's, from
the site's own table. The barley gap needs a different explanation.

What does survive from that analysis: the parameters the Morris screen ranks most influential
(`alfa.bm_e` μ\* = 112, `barl.bm_e` 25.3, `corn.lai_pot` 18.6) are exactly the ones in this
table. So the model's most sensitive values are site-calibrated, which is the good case — but
it also means the sensitivity screen was ranking someone else's calibration, and the paper
should say so.

The table also anchors the Stage-3 fit: the optimizer pushed corn `bm_e` to **59.7** against a
site-calibrated 50.0 and a SWAT default of 39.0. It is not merely above the literature — it is
well above a value that was already calibrated upward for this site.

The companion sheet `Plant Harvest Dates` gives per-crop emergence, up to four harvest dates,
and **irrigation start/end windows** (alfalfa 4/15–10/09, spring barley 4/15–7/25, corn silage
5/25–9/18). These are the field's documented irrigation windows and are the natural bounds for
the gym's auto-irrigation lever; they are **not yet used**.

### Harvest indices — checked, and correct

`harv.ops` carries corn 0.98, barley 0.52, alfalfa 0.95 at efficiency 1.0. The spreadsheet's
`HARV_EFF` column (0.98 / 0.54 / 0.95) invites a transcription error, but the reference's
`000140001.mgt` settles it: its harvest operations read `HI_OVR 0.98 / HARVEFF 1.00` for corn
and `HI_OVR 0.52 / HARVEFF 1.00` for barley. Our values match the reference exactly.

## 4. The containing-object areas — set by us, and previously wrong

`hru.con` was resized to 1 ha, but the objects *containing* the HRU kept A10's **1431.72 ha** —
above all `object.cnt`'s `ls_area`/`tot_area`, which is what SWAT+ normalises basin-level tables
by. Every such table was diluted ~1432×: aquifer recharge read 0.04 mm against 62 mm of soil
percolation, nitrate to groundwater ~0.1 kg/ha.

Nothing was mis-simulated — the reported units were not per-field. But `basin_aqu_yr.no3_rchg`
is the leaching term in the gym's reward, where it would have read a constant zero and made a
leaching ablation meaningless. Fixed in `port_soil.py::resize_containing_objects`. Side effect:
the engine stopped routing a 1432 ha channel and every run path got ~3× faster.

|---|---|---|
| 15 | 16.68 | 53.35 |
| 31 | 20.75 | 6.88 |
| 61 | 7.33 | 1.88 |

## 5d. `ET Calibration/` — re-audited 2026-07-28; the earlier verdict was wrong

This section previously read "checked, and not applicable". That was half right and materially
misleading, and it was written from a shallow look. The corrected account:

**What these files are.** Seven per-crop workbooks (alfalfa, barley, corn, beans, potato,
sugarbeet, wheat) plus a plots workbook, 41 sheets in all. `rev693` is **SWAT+ revision 69.3**,
not a spreadsheet revision. Each workbook pairs OpenET/eeMetric satellite ET against a SWAT+
69.3 simulation for **three commercial fields per crop, 21 fields in total**, 2020–2022. There
are **no crop coefficients, no Kcb curves, no growth-stage heat-unit fractions, no rooting
depths and no lysimeter ET** anywhere in the folder — that much of the old verdict holds, and
it is why this cannot be a tier-1 ET target.

**Three things the old verdict got wrong.**

1. *The coordinates it listed were only the barley fields.* There are 21 distinct fields, three
   per crop, all within ~8 km of the GRACEnet plot on the same Portneuf silt loam under the
   same AgriMet TWFI climate. Alfalfa 42.5438/−114.2740, 42.5436/−114.2734, 42.5135/−114.3789;
   corn 42.5444/−114.3182, 42.5441/−114.3134, 42.4940/−114.3786.
2. *"Not the simulated years" is false for one year in three.* The `OpenET 2020` sheets are a
   daily ET series for a real Kimberly field in a year the model used to simulate — and used to
   simulate as a fabrication. That year has since been dropped (see `port_management.SIM_END_YEAR`).
3. *The satellite ET is not the main asset.* The embedded **SWAT+ 69.3 daily output** —
   ET, LAI, biomass, N/P uptake, irrigation and the four stress terms — is a peer model of the

## 5e. Long-Term Manure study — the barley diagnosis

`USDA Long-Term Manure/` is a **separate experiment on the same site**: different plots (505,
606, 701, 808), a different rotation (barley / sugarbeet / wheat / potato) and heavier manure
(551–952 kg N/ha/yr). Not a source of inputs for this model. It is a source of *evidence*.

### Measured whole-plant barley biomass settles what the barley gap is

`LT Manure Soil Properties and P uptake data.xlsx`, sheet `plant P uptake`:

| year | plots | grain (Mg/ha) | whole-plant DM (Mg/ha) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 4 | 5.66 – 6.98 | **12.8 – 15.9** |
| 2017 | 4 | — | **13.5 – 20.4** |
| | mean | 6.4 | **15.6** |

Against this model's 2014 barley: peak standing biomass **4.5 Mg/ha**, harvested 1.87 Mg/ha.

Three conclusions, and they close a question that has been open since the reconciliation:

1. **Barley biomass is roughly 3× too low, and that is the whole gap.** Measured whole-plant DM
   is ~14–15 Mg/ha; the model peaks at 4.5. The earlier inference from the reference model
   ("~12.5 t/ha implied") was right and slightly conservative.
2. **Harvest index is not the problem.** Measured grain / whole-plant DM implies HI ≈ **0.36**
   once grain is corrected to dry matter from the recorded 14.5 % moisture (5.10/14.05 and
   6.14/17.05); an earlier reading here said 0.41–0.45 by skipping that correction. The model
   uses 0.52, i.e. it is *too high*, so correcting HI would widen the gap rather than close it.
   Applying the model's 0.52 to a correct biomass would give ~7.3 Mg/ha of grain, inside the
   measured 5.9–8.8 range: the model would produce approximately the right yield if it grew
   approximately the right plant. It now grows a larger one (§5f) but still not a large enough
   one.
3. **The GRACEnet barley target is grain, and it is consistent.** LT grain averages 6.4 Mg/ha;
   the GRACEnet plot reports 5.90 (2014) and 8.78 (2019). Same quantity, same magnitude — so
   the −69.9 % barley PBIAS is a real biomass failure, not a plant-part mismatch.

`LT Manure Data Summary.xlsx`, sheet `Soil N & P vs SWAT` — annual pre-plant profile NO₃-N and
0–30 cm P with standard deviations, 2013–2020, alongside that study's SWAT values. Different
plots and manure regime, so not a target for this model, but it is a measured soil-nutrient
*trajectory* at this site and the closest thing available to validation for the N and P pools.

**The GRACEnet workbook has the equivalent series for *this* field**, and that one *is* a
target as of 2026-07-31 — see §5i. The LT Manure series above remains context only.

## 5f. The full-folder audit, 2026-07-28 — what it changed

Every file in `~/Documents/Kimberly, Idaho/` was checked against the model on disk, one auditor
per source directory, verifying against the built `TxtInOut` rather than against this document
or the porting scripts' docstrings. That distinction mattered: several long-standing provenance
claims turned out to be false.

### The barley shortfall was a plant-type bug

Diagnosed and fixed. The chain:

1. `Plant Harvest Dates` documents **1800 heat units** for Spring Barley (and for Corn Silage;
   2000 for potato and winter wheat, 2300 sugarbeet, 1000 pinto beans). **That column was
   ported nowhere.** `management.sch` writes `hu_sch = 0.0` on all 185 ops, so SWAT+ derives
   the target from `plants.plt days_mat` — and `days_mat` does not exist in the site's crop
   table at all, so the model was running the SWAT+ editor's stock 105.
2. But `days_mat` was not the cause. Barley was typed **`cold_annual`** (from the sheet's
   `IDC = 5`), and SWAT+ rev 60.5.7's cold-annual pathway is built for a fall-sown crop that
   banks heat units before dormancy. Applied to April-sown spring barley it collapsed the
   target to ~530 degree-days: the crop matured **46 days after planting**, then stood in the
   field for another 66–85 days with biomass frozen to the kilogram and every stress term at
   exactly 0.000 until harvest.
3. Setting `plnt_typ = warm_annual`, changing nothing else, restores the growth window to 97
   and 110 days and realizes **1517 and 1766** heat units — within 6–16 % of the documented
   1800, with no tuning.

**Corn is the control.** It is already `warm_annual` and already realizes 1889/1888 against the
same documented 1800, at its own stock `days_mat 120`. The method reproduces the source value
for the crop that was never broken, which is what makes the barley result a fix rather than a
coincidence.

`days_mat` is deliberately left at 105. It has no source value, and moving it trades heat-unit
fidelity against yield fit — calibration, not porting.

**What this did and did not solve.** Barley went from −69.9 % to **−56.2 %** against
measurement. It did not close the gap. With the phenology corrected, 2014 barley runs
*stress-free* — no water, N or temperature stress — and is limited purely by radiation
interception with LAI at its `lai_pot` ceiling of 4.0. The residual is therefore radiation-use
efficiency (`bm_e` 28.0, the site's calibrated value; the sheet's own default block says 35.0)
and the LAI curve, both of which are calibration questions with the porting question now
settled. A grid fit of the LAI development fractions at a forced 1800-HU target reached
3.82/4.56 t/ha against 5.90/8.78 with the optimum on the grid boundary — better, but not
applied, because it is a fit and no bounds decision has been taken (§6).

## 5h. `pet_co` was the wrong knob — the ET/PET ratio is the discrepancy

`pet_co = 0.81` entered the model on 2026-07-31 as a **provisional drainage restore**: under
rev 62 deep percolation fell to ~0, taking `no3_rchg` with it, and scaling PET down bought
back ~33 mm/yr. That is fitting a PET parameter to a percolation symptom.

`scripts/calibrate_petco.py` calibrates it against the one measurement that speaks to it: the
AgriMet TWFI **grass**-reference ET series (`ETOS`), which is the definition SWAT+'s
Penman–Monteith PET matches. (`ETRS`, alfalfa-reference, runs ~35 % higher; calibrating
against it would be a definitional error.) PET is exactly linear in `pet_co` — four sweep
points imply the same optimum to four decimals — so:

**`pet_co* = 0.964`, giving PET bias +0.0 % against measured ETos.** The incumbent 0.81 scored
−16.0 %. It was not a mild correction; it was pushing PET a sixth below the measurement.

The cost is that the drainage signal does not survive the correction:

| `pet_co` | PET vs measured ETos | deep perc | `no3_rchg` | yield vs GRACEnet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.81 (was) | −16.0 % | 42.6 mm/yr | 1.12 kg/ha/yr | −6.7 % |
| 0.90 | −6.6 % | 8.1 | 0.26 | −7.0 % |
| **0.964** | **+0.0 %** | **1.1** | **0.02** | −9.8 % |
| 1.00 | +3.8 % | 0.3 | 0.01 | −11.6 % |

So the honest reading is that **there is no percolation to restore.** Two facts locate the
problem, and neither is PET:

1. `perco`, the SWAT+ coefficient that governs exactly this step, is inert — full-range sweep
   moves percolation 1.1 → 2.8 mm/yr. Water is not being held above field capacity and denied
   passage; it is gone before it gets there.
2. At the PET-matched `pet_co`, actual ET is 835 mm against the reference's 711 mm. The
   reference **over**-predicts PET by +20.8 % against the same measured ETos, and still
   evaporates less water. The ET/PET ratio is ~0.86 here against ~0.51 there.

The parameters that set that ratio — `esco` 0.95 and `epco` 0.50 — already match REF exactly,
and so does the soil profile that supplies the water. The divergence is therefore **between
the engines**, SWAT+ rev 62 against SWAT2012 rev 693, not between the inputs. That is a
finding about the port, and it belongs in the manuscript as one; it should not be absorbed
into a PET multiplier.

**Consequence for the experiments, stated plainly.** `no3_rchg` is now ~0.02 kg/ha/yr and is
no longer an informative variable. `CLAUDE.md` rule 1 requires nitrate leaching to be reported
alongside profit; at this magnitude that report is a row of zeros. Exp 2's leaching column
needs either the ET gap resolved first, or an explicit statement that the model cannot resolve
leaching at this site under rev 62. **This is open — see `OPEN_ITEMS.md` #11.**

> inert", and the claim that the leaching column is a row of zeros. See `OPEN_ITEMS.md` #11.

## 5i. The measured soil-nitrate trajectory, 2026-07-31 — and why it does not close

`GraceNet Soil and Nutrient Properties.xlsx::Soil N & P` holds a **seven-year April soil-nitrate
profile on this field** — four plots at five depths (15/31/61/91/122 cm), 2013–2019. §5c used
its 2013 column for the initial condition and §5a listed the rest as "extracted but not
applied". It is now extracted in full (`scripts/extract_primary.soil_no3` →
`data/soil_no3_gracenet.csv`) and scored (`scripts/n_trajectory.py`).

It is the **only measured constraint on the nitrogen cycle at this site**, which matters
because §5h left the leaching term at ~0.02 kg/ha/yr with no measurement to judge it against.

| April | 2013 | 2014 | 2015 | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| profile NO₃-N, kg/ha | 159.0 | 237.8 | 116.4 | 68.5 | 97.4 | 214.9 | 226.8 |
| plot-to-plot SD, ppm | 7.46 | 4.83 | 2.13 | 0.93 | 2.55 | 7.65 | 7.05 |

The sampled profile is *exactly* the modelled profile — soil `80295` is four layers to 1220 mm
at the same measured bulk densities — so no depth reconciliation is needed, and the 2013 value
reproduces the ported `nutrients.sol:nitrate` of 9.32983 ppm to five decimals.

### The model side has to be reconstructed

**SWAT+ rev 62 does not print the soil nitrate pool.** This was checked exhaustively, not
assumed: every `no3` column across all ~24 output tables is either a flux or a store belonging
to another object, and enabling the entire `hru_cb_*` family adds nothing — `hru_soil_snap`
carries physical properties only and `hru_ncycle` carries rates. So the pool is accumulated
from the fluxes that enter and leave it.

That balance closes except for one term. The GRACEnet manures are 20 % mineral N and that
mineral fraction is 99 % NH₃-N, and rev 62 prints neither the nitrification flux nor the
volatilisation loss, so the fate of applied ammonium is invisible. Rather than assume it, the
reconstruction **brackets** it — all volatilised at one end, all nitrified at the other — and
scores only the distance *outside* the bracket. The three alfalfa years receive no manure, so
there the bracket collapses to a point and the balance is exact.

### Three findings, in increasing order of how much they constrain the science

**1. `rsd_decomp` is inert, and the manure organic N never reaches the fresh pool.** A full
sweep (0.05 → 0.80) leaves every output identical to the digit. In SWAT2012 the reference
routes manure N through a fast fresh-residue pool — its `F_MN` is 24–164 kg N/ha/yr — but here
`rsd_nitorg_n` is exactly 0.000 in all seven years. All 80 % of applied manure N that is
organic goes to the slow humus pool instead.

**2. At the same nominal `CMN`, SWAT+ mineralises 2–4× less than SWAT2012.** §5e accepted
`orgn_min = 0.0010` because the LT Manure sweep found `cmn = 0.001` reproduced the measured
buried-bag rate of 209.8 kg N/ha/yr to +4.7 %. That sweep was run **in SWAT2012**. The same
0.0010 in this model yields **54.4 kg N/ha/yr**, and the ArcSWAT reference's humus term
(`A_MN`) on *this* field averages 117. The parameter value did not transfer across engines,
which is the same class of finding as §5h.

**3. Alfalfa fixation makes three of the seven years structurally unreachable.** SWAT computes
legume fixation as the shortfall between demand and soil supply, so soil nitrate and fixation
are strict substitutes:

| | mineralised | fixed |
|---|---|---|
| as built | 60 / 52 / 49 | 122 / 330 / 301 |
| `orgn_min` 0.003 | 133 / 99 / 83 | 0 / 225 / 269 |
| `fr_hum_act` 0.40 | 231 / 195 / 192 | 0 / 0 / 0 |

Every extra kilogram mineralised is a kilogram not fixed, and uptake absorbs the difference, so
the soil pool stays where it was. The measurement has the pool **rising** +29 and +117 kg/ha in
two alfalfa years. No parameter setting reproduces that; it would require mineralisation to
exceed alfalfa's entire N demand. Those three years therefore carry an irreducible miss, which
is why the nitrate term is down-weighted in `scripts/calibrate/optimize.py` — for a structural
reason, not because the measurement is doubted. The four annual-crop years have no fixation and
are genuinely informative.

## 5j. Dead knobs the fit exposed, 2026-07-31

A first pass at fitting to the measurements returned a parameter set with `corn.harv_idx`
pinned at its lower bound and `corn.bm_e` at 64.6 against a literature ~39–45. That reads as a
degenerate ridge between two parameters that multiply into yield. It was not. One-at-a-time
sweeps across the **full** bound range of each suspect gave:

| parameter | swept | effect on corn / alfa / barl PBIAS |
|---|---|---|
| `plants.plt:corn.harv_idx` | 0.713 → 1.313 | **bit-identical** |
| `hydrology.hyd:latq_co` | 0.0 → 1.0 | **bit-identical** on yield; ET 834.8 → 834.7 |
| `hydrology.hyd:cn3_swf` | 0.0 → 1.0 | −11.2 → −10.9, i.e. 0.3 points across the whole range |
| `plants.plt:corn.bm_e` | 35 → 65 | −22.9 → −5.7 — genuinely live |

**`plants.plt:harv_idx` is inert for every crop in this rotation.** All three are harvested by
`hvkl` against a `harv.ops` row whose `harv_typ` is `biomass`, and that path takes the harvest
index from `harv.ops`, not from `plants.plt`. The operative values are `gn_corn` 0.98,
`gn_barl` 0.54, `gn_alfa` 0.95 — already tagged `reference_HI_override`, so they came from REF.
The fit had been tuning a column the engine never reads, and differential evolution parked the
dead dimension against a bound, where it looked exactly like a meaningful pin.

Three consequences, all applied:

- `harv.ops` is now in `params.CALIBRATABLE`, and the three `gn_*` rows are the harvest-index
  parameters the fit actually moves.
- `plants.plt:harv_idx`, `latq_co` and `cn3_swf` are dropped from the free set, alongside the
  already-known inert `perco` and `rsd_decomp`.
- **A parameter at a bound is not evidence of a binding constraint until it is shown to be
  live.** `PROVENANCE` §6 and `README` previously cited "four of thirteen parameters optimize
  to their bounds" as grounds for rejecting a fit; at least one of those four was a dead knob,
  so that argument was weaker than it read.

## 6. Fitted parameters — fifteen, and what each one bought

Two separate fits, against two different tier-1 measurements.

**`hydrology.hyd:pet_co = 0.964`**, fitted to measured AgriMet grass-reference ETos by
`scripts/calibrate_petco.py` (§5h). A one-parameter fit against a measurement with a
closed-form solution and no free choices — the weakest kind of fitting there is, but it is
fitting. It replaced a value fitted to a *simulated* quantity, which was worse. It is **not**
in the free set below: CLAUDE.md rule 10 keeps the yield objective from pulling on it.

**Fourteen parameters fitted to GRACEnet yield and soil nitrate** by
`scripts/calibrate/optimize.py`, applied 2026-07-31 (`runs/calibration.json`, 15,616
evaluations). The objective is **per-year** yield error over all seven rotation years plus the
soil-nitrate trajectory of §5i, with PET held as a bound.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| yield, RMS per-year error | 25.5 % | **13.6 %** |
| yield, PBIAS (bias only) | −9.8 % | **−3.0 %** |
| corn / alfalfa / barley PBIAS | −10.9 / −12.9 / +2.3 | **−7.0 / +2.2 / −4.9** |
| PET vs measured ETos | +0.0 % | +0.0 % |
| soil-nitrate RMS miss | 91.4 kg/ha | 94.4 kg/ha |

> ⚠️ **Superseded 2026-08-28 — the "after" column describes a model that is no longer on disk,
> and deliberately so.** Rule 11b (decided 2026-08-07, six days after this fit) returned the
> crop coefficients to the collaborator's workbook, which reverts the six ``bm_e``/``lai_pot``
> entries and the three ``harv.ops`` harvest indices this fit had moved. That reversion was
> applied to barley and alfalfa but **not to corn**, so between 2026-08-07 and 2026-08-28 the
> model matched neither the fit nor the workbook: corn still carried ``bm_e = 64.5`` and
> ``lai_pot = 4.95`` — the value rule 11b names explicitly — plus three canopy-curve columns
> retired in ``port_crops.py``'s comments and never undone in the file. Reconciled 2026-08-28
> and now enforced by ``scripts/check_param_state.py`` and ``tests/test_param_state.py``.
>
> **The model as it now stands**, measured by ``scripts/calib_report.py``:
>
> | | fit as reported above | on disk 2026-08-28 |
> |---|---|---|
> | yield, RMS per-year | 13.6 % | **42.3 %** |
> | yield, PBIAS | −3.0 % | **+9.3 %** |
> | corn / alfalfa / barley | −7.0 / +2.2 / −4.9 | **−24.6 / +49.5 / +3.9** |
> | PET vs measured ETos | +0.0 % | +0.0 % |
> | soil-nitrate RMS, all years | 94.4 kg/ha | 90.9 kg/ha |
>
> This is the cost rule 11b priced and accepted — "book values keep the bias legible" — but it
> is a much larger cost than the 2.3 points of mean |PBIAS| the rule quotes, because alfalfa's
> ``bm_e`` reverting 12.25 → 17.0 leaves it **+49.5 %** over measurement. Two consequences
> follow and neither is settled: any yield-dependent result must be read under a +49.5 %
> alfalfa bias, and if the fit is ever re-run it must exclude the workbook columns (which
> ``PARAMS`` already does) so that the two writers cannot diverge again.

Nine move within a **±30 % window around the site's own calibrated table** — `bm_e` and
`lai_pot` for each crop, and the three `harv.ops` harvest indices. Five had no source value at
all: `alfa.lai_min`, `corn`/`barl.days_mat`, `epco`, `orgn_min`.

**Two parameters the fit moved were held back at their sourced values, and the model is better
for it.** `n_perc` optimized to 0.9935 against REF's 0.20 and `fr_hum_act` to its floor; both
were buying ~6 kg/ha on the nitrate term while making yield *worse*. Held at 0.20 and 0.02, the
yield RMS is 13.6 % rather than 14.1 % and corn is −7.0 rather than −9.1. `n_perc` is in any
case near-inert here — 0.01 → 1.00 moves corn by 0.2 points.

### What is not honest to claim about this fit

- **`corn.bm_e = 64.5` is not a radiation-use efficiency.** It is +29 % on the site's
  calibrated 50.0 and well above the literature ~39–45, it sits at the top of its window, and
  corn *still* lands at −7.0 %. It is absorbing the documented resident-perennial artefact
  (`plant.ini` keeps alfalfa competing for light in every corn year), not measuring anything.
  Treat corn's agreement as fitted, not validated.
- **The nitrate trajectory did not improve and could not have.** §5i explains why; the term is
  down-weighted for that structural reason.
- **Barley remains the scattered crop**: +23.5 % in 2014 against −24.1 % in 2019. The per-crop
  −4.9 % is cancellation, which is exactly why the objective is per-year.

Every other value in the model is measured, taken from REF, taken from RuFaS, set from site
geometry, or an A10 default. **Nothing else has been tuned to fit an outcome.**
